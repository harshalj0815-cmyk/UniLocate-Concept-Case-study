| Industry, Product, Type | Indoor Navigation + Education, UniLocate (Type C) |
|  User | A 19-year-old student arrives at a new campus for an exam. She can’t find her exam hall, and the people she asks don’t know either. With about 15 minutes left, she opens UniLocate to find her way. She doesn’t want to be late or waste time signing up. |
| AARRR | Activation (Primary). |
| Flow | She scans the QR code on her admit card (or opens the college link) > opens UniLocate. She skips sign-up and picks the campus. > She searches her venue, like “Exam Hall B”.  > She follows the indoor route, with floors, stairs and lifts shown clearly. > She arrives, and the app then asks her to save her account. |
| Provisional Metric | - % of first-time visitors who reach their venue without help in their first session. Second metric:<br/>- % of those who arrive and then create an account. |
| Provisional Hypothesis | By allowing users to skip sign-up and get a route in 3 taps (with clear floor/stair indicators and offline functionality), more visitors will reach their venue on the first try. Once the app has provided value, they will be more likely to create an account because the value is proven and the initial time pressure is gone. |
| Scope Sentence | I am designing a first-visit route helper for UniLocate for 18 to 22 year old students visiting an unfamiliar campus, targeting Activation, to improve the % who reach their venue in the first session. I believe that when visitors can scan a QR code, skip sign-up, and get a clear, offline-ready indoor route in 3 taps, more of them will arrive on the first try. After they arrive, they will be more willing to make an account. |
